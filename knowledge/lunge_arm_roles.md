# Sword arm and non-sword arm in a simple lunge

The weapon arm, also called the sword arm, is the arm holding the fencing weapon.
In the conventional simple-lunge teaching sequence, this arm reaches forward toward
the target with the point aimed at it. It is not extended sideways for balance.
The rear or non-weapon arm is the other arm. In the conventional teaching finish,
it extends back behind the fencer, separately from the weapon arm. Do not prescribe
a shoulder-width distance between an arm and the body: that is not this instruction.
Exact non-weapon-arm positions vary with style and action; this is general instruction,
not a finding that an athlete's recorded arm position is faulty.

When someone asks which arm extends toward the target, identify the sword arm first.
Do not infer right or left without knowing which hand holds the weapon. A clarification
should resolve the ambiguity and correct a previously wrong instruction rather than
repeat it. Changing sides does not make the arms interchangeable in their roles.

bout timing, a medical recommendation, or a validated scoring threshold.

## Questions about angles

An elbow's bend and an arm's direction relative to the floor or torso are different
angles. These references teach extending toward the chosen target and a conventional
rear-arm finish; they do not establish a universal numeric shoulder-elevation target.
Answer the direction question concretely: sword arm toward the target, non-sword arm
behind in the traditional lunge finish. Target height changes the aiming direction.
Do not turn an observed elbow measurement into an ideal angle for the whole arm.
