## Evidence details and values excluded from app proposals

**S1 — Dedieu et al., 2024, DOI 10.7752/jpes.2024.04111.** Sixteen foil and sixteen épée national-level competitors, more than five years' experience; ten-camera **laboratory 3D Vicon at 200 Hz**. These are national competitors, not a claimed sample of world-elite athletes. Stabilisation runs from armed-foot contact to maximum armed-side hip flexion. Full text linked by the cited HAL record: [same paper PDF](https://cnrs.hal.science/hal-04788048v1/file/Dedieu%20etal%202024%20JPES.pdf). Table 2 was visually inspected. Phase means must not be treated as individual endpoint limits.

**S2 — Klinger, Adrian and Dee, Konstanz pre-lunge paper.** Methods, p. 210: three elite **male** fencers, despite the title identifying female participants; perpendicular **2D film, 200 frames/s**, not 3D capture. Table II, p. 211 reports durations in milliseconds by condition 1/2/3: subject 1 **400 / 400 / 250**; subject 2 **550 / 500 / 400**; subject 3 **500 / 550 / 600**. Interval: lead-ankle forward movement to lead-foot landing. No combined range or average calculated. Results, p. 212 reports approximately **180°** for subject 1 and maximum **135°** for subject 2 during lead-leg extension, **not at finish**. Recovery hyperextension is described without a numerical recovery angle. Table II was visually inspected.

**S3 — Corrêa et al., 2015, coach-criteria paper.** One skilled amateur boy, age eleven; **laboratory 3D Optitrack, eighteen cameras, 120 Hz**, not elite adults. Table 1, p. 826, target/no-target mean ± SEM: armed-elbow ROM **68 ± 2° / 73 ± 5°**; attack period **0.98 ± 0.02 s / 1.03 ± 0.03 s**; peak CM anterior–posterior velocity **1.74 ± 0.01 m/s / 1.88 ± 0.01 m/s**. Methods, pp. 824–825 define attack period by pelvis/CM velocity increasing until it reaches zero. These are ROM, attack duration and whole-body peak speed, not finish elbow angle, recovery duration or torso-normalised hip speed. Table 1 was visually inspected; plotted endpoints were not estimated.

**S4 — Turner et al., 2016, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001402.** Thirty-six elite senior/junior male fencers. Methods, Repeat Lunge Ability, and Table 1, p. 3136: **16.03 s (SD 1.40 s)** for the circuit score, with Brower timing gates, not 2D or 3D joint capture. Multiple hits and backward/forward travel are included. No division into an inferred single-lunge or recovery time. The original Swansea record and download return 404; the **same paper** was checked via its [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26950343/) and [author full-text copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309619762_PHYSICAL_CHARACTERISTICS_UNDERPINNING_REPETITIVE_LUNGING_IN_FENCING), not a new study.

**S5 — Boyd and Milton, The Undervalued Lunge.** PTQ 4.4, p. 45, lowering-phase teaching cues and Table 1 prescribe approximately **90°** knee bending / a **90°** lower-position condition. This is a **general strength-training lunge tutorial**, not measured fencing finish data, a recruited population, or a 2D/3D observational experiment. It is explicitly excluded as a fencing target. The cited legacy host returns 403; the identical article was retrieved from [NSCA's official current host](https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/24dd7222ed1b4caeb8a0a46b81bd11f3/ptq-4.4.9-the-undervalued-lunge.pdf). No papers in its bibliography were added to this search.

**S6 — Kim et al., 2015, muscle-imbalance training.** Nine elite male Korean national fencers; laboratory marker-based motion capture, infrared cameras at 200 Hz and force platforms at 1,000 Hz. Methods/Results concern single-leg standing, deep squats and balance-scale tasks, **not fencing finish or recovery measurements**. Direct PMC access was challenged/blocked; its cached indexed article text was inspected. A public NCBI full-text endpoint returned 429 and was not retried. No numerical fencing reference was extracted.
