# Basic solo lunge review rubric

For a conventional straight lunge toward a fixed torso-height target, review
front-leg bend, rear-leg extension, rear-foot contact, torso control and weapon-arm
direction together. A classical teaching model uses approximately a right-angle
front knee at the finish, a straightened rear leg, a planted rear sole and an erect
torso. This is a drill reference, not a universal correctness threshold for bouts.
Aim the weapon hand toward the actual target; roughly horizontal extension fits
a target near hand height. A different target or athlete can justify a different
arm direction. Do not equate elbow angle with arm direction relative to the floor.

User's chosen practice rubric: aim for about 90 degrees or somewhat wider at the
front knee, rear-leg extension, grounded rear foot, upright torso, and an arm
directed at target height. Treat these as the intended drill, not learned universal
pass/fail limits. Knee-over-toe position is a separate geometric measurement from
the knee's included angle. A 90-degree angle alone cannot establish it. Neither
can pose landmarks establish ground contact force, explosiveness or injury risk.
Do not prescribe a perfectly vertical torso throughout every competitive action.

Use submitted feedback for the particular reviewed rep, attributing the finding
to its reviewer. A reference-rated rep with a stated issue is not a perfect rep.
Intentional errors remain errors for this drill, but must not be called accidental
habits. Conflicting target descriptions (torso versus eye height) call for asking
which target is intended, rather than declaring either one correct.
